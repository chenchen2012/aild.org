## What changed this week

- Nearly half of large enterprises lack full visibility into employee AI use (Protiviti survey).
- Anthropic's latest model exposes gaps in corporate governance, prompting a new framework for CEOs.
- ServiceNow Knowledge 2026 emphasizes that enterprise AI implementation needs guardrails and control.
- OneStream reports companies are scaling AI on data they don't trust, highlighting data integrity issues.
- Qlik and Deloitte argue enterprise AI success depends on more than models—operating model and data trust matter.

## Leadership implications

- Executives must develop AI fluency to make informed decisions, not just adopt tools (source 1).
- AI is spreading decision-making but not accountability; leaders must clarify ownership and risk (source 2).
- Governing data exposure is a silent risk layer; CEOs need frameworks for generative AI (source 3).
- A unified platform and operating model are foundational for AI scalability (source 6).
- CIOs must ask three critical questions before signing AI contracts to avoid vendor lock-in and hidden risks (source 12).

## Decisions for the next 7 days

- Audit current AI usage across the organization to identify shadow AI and data exposure risks.
- Establish a cross-functional AI governance committee with clear accountability for decisions.
- Define a single AI platform and operating model to consolidate tools and ensure data trust.
- Require AI fluency training for all executives and decision-makers.
- Review all AI vendor contracts against a checklist of governance, data rights, and exit clauses.
