## What changed this week

- Enterprise AI adoption is surging, but data governance is not keeping up, creating significant risk (Source 3).
- Agentic AI workflows are moving from pilots to production, promising autonomous decision-making but requiring new guardrails (Source 4, 10).
- A new report reveals 8 out of 10 CEOs worldwide believe AI could cost them their job, highlighting leadership anxiety (Source 7).
- Edge AI is gaining traction, shifting from centralized cloud to distributed, real-time processing (Source 11).
- FICO World 2026 will showcase AI innovations, emphasizing decision management and operational AI (Source 5).

## Leadership implications

- Boards must ask tough questions about AI oversight, responsible use, and alignment with strategy (Source 8).
- IT leadership is at a 'make-or-break' moment: failing to adapt operating models for AI will undermine competitiveness (Source 6).
- ROI from AI remains elusive for many; leaders must shift focus from model deployment to measurable business outcomes (Source 2).
- Enterprise AI success depends on more than models—it requires robust data governance, change management, and a culture of experimentation (Source 1, 12).
- The rise of agentic AI demands new governance frameworks like AEGIS to ensure safety and control (Source 10).

## Decisions for the next 7 days

- Assess your organization's AI readiness: evaluate data governance maturity and identify gaps (Source 3, 9).
- Initiate a board-level discussion on AI oversight, using the 10 questions from the responsible AI framework (Source 8).
- Review your AI portfolio for ROI: kill or pivot projects that lack clear business impact metrics (Source 2).
- Start a pilot for agentic AI workflows with strict guardrails, focusing on low-risk, high-value processes (Source 4, 10).
- Communicate to leadership teams about the CEO anxiety trend and reinforce the importance of AI upskilling and change management (Source 7, 12).
