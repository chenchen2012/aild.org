## What changed this week

- Senior leaders are the biggest source of shadow AI risk, with 60% of unauthorized AI use originating from executives (TrustedTech Global).
- Persistent and IIM Ahmedabad launched the 'AI Value Compass' to bring discipline and measurable outcomes to enterprise AI investments.
- Kore.ai introduced Artemis, a platform to build, govern, and optimize enterprise AI agents, highlighting the need for centralized governance.
- Malaysia announced a move from AI pilots to national-scale transformation, signaling a shift in government AI strategy.
- New research shows AI-driven change is accelerating, with the technology executive role expanding under pressure to lead AI strategy.

## Leadership implications

- Executives must model responsible AI use to mitigate shadow AI risk; governance policies should apply equally to leadership.
- AI governance frameworks like the 'AI Value Compass' can help align investments with business outcomes and reduce waste.
- CIOs and CHROs must collaborate to retain AI talent, as competition for skilled professionals intensifies.
- Human judgment remains critical in AI operations, especially for network management and decision-making in uncertain contexts.
- Unbudgeted AI costs (e.g., data infrastructure, compliance, retraining) can derail ROI; leaders should plan for total cost of ownership.

## Decisions for the next 7 days

- Audit current AI usage across the organization to identify shadow AI and establish clear governance policies.
- Evaluate the 'AI Value Compass' or similar frameworks to bring discipline to AI investment decisions.
- Initiate a joint CIO-CHRO task force to develop a talent retention plan for AI and data science roles.
- Review AI project portfolios to shift from pilot purgatory to scaled, measurable impact.
- Budget for hidden AI costs including data quality, model monitoring, and regulatory compliance.
